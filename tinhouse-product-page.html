Why the adaptation sounds like your book

It reads your novel before it writes a single scene.

Adaptation goes wrong when a tool reads chapter by chapter, guessing what came before. Character voices flatten. Subplots drop out. Themes get quietly replaced with genre defaults. That's the reason most AI screenplays feel generic.

FluidScriptr works differently. Before it writes a single line of screenplay, it builds a Story Bible — a complete map of your characters, arcs, voice, tone, and themes. Every scene it produces is anchored to that map, so nothing drifts.

Think of it the way you'd think of a great adapter: someone who reads your entire novel, understands what makes it yours, and then translates it — faithfully — into a screenplay. That's not replacing your creativity. That's respecting it.

Common questions

Things writers ask.

What's the catch with the free 10 pages?
There isn't one. Upload a chapter, and FluidScriptr converts the first 10 pages using the same engine paying customers get — same Story Bible pass, same screenplay formatting. No card, no account, no trial that quietly starts charging. We ask for your email so we can send you the finished file, and so you can find it again later. Ten pages isn't a screenplay, so it won't adapt your book for you — but it's more than enough to see whether the writing holds up before you spend anything.
Does FluidScriptr work for full-length novels?
Yes — it handles complete manuscripts with no chapter limits and no naive chunking that loses continuity. The Story Bible is built from the whole book before any screenplay is written, so voice and character stay consistent from the first scene to the last. If you don't have a manuscript yet, you can also answer six guided questions to generate one from an idea, or upload existing pages to enhance.
How faithful is the adaptation?
As faithful as the source material lets it be — and it is worth being precise about what that means. Your dialogue carries over close to verbatim wherever it plays on screen, and the Story Bible locks your characters, voice, arcs and themes before generation starts. What gets written fresh is the action and description, because a novel has no action lines to convert — a narrator's interior observation has to become something an audience can actually watch. So expect your story and your dialogue back, plus new connective prose in the stage directions. You also get to review and edit the Bible before conversion — the creative decisions stay with you.
What is the Story Bible?
The Story Bible is a structured analysis of your story — characters, arcs, voice, tone, and themes — built before any output is generated. Every scene FluidScriptr writes is anchored to this map, so nothing drifts and your voice stays consistent from page one to the end.
Which output formats does FluidScriptr support?
Screenplays export in Fountain, Final Draft (.fdx), and formatted PDF. Manuscripts export as DOCX, PDF, and RTF — Times New Roman, double-spaced, one-inch margins — submission-ready from the first export.
Do you store or train on my manuscript?
No. Your uploads are never retained and your work is never used for model training. With included credits, requests pass through our servers to Anthropic but are not stored — we keep only anonymous usage counts. If you use your own API key, it's stored only in your browser and requests go directly to Anthropic. Either way, what you write stays yours.
What AI model does FluidScriptr use?
FluidScriptr's conversions are powered by Claude Sonnet (Anthropic) — the same top-tier model either way. Every license includes monthly generation credits, enough for roughly two full projects a month, no setup required. Want unlimited generation? Add your own Anthropic API key in the app and pay Anthropic directly — typically a few dollars per full conversion.
